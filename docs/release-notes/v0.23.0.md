Warbell v0.23.0 improves late-game navigation and large armies, and brings the rebuilt first-person view into a public release.

## Performance — late nights and large armies

- Failed or finished routes no longer trigger a new path search every frame. Orks, guards, workers and skirmish armies respect their retry deadlines, including when a destination cannot be reached.
- Large group orders spread route planning across several frames. Units begin moving immediately, with expensive navigation staggered over at most 150 ms.
- Navigation reuses search buffers instead of rebuilding hash tables for every unit. Frozen CPU workloads produced identical routes with substantially less search time; these are navigation measurements, not a promised FPS multiplier.
- Troops and workers with identical outfits share their meshes and neutral material. Large armies keep independent animation and outfit variety while avoiding repeated mesh uploads. The outfit cache has a fixed limit.
- Guards, soldiers and watchtowers look for nearby targets through an adaptive index. Small or tightly packed battles use a contiguous scan, while spread-out forces use spatial buckets. Target priority, sight ranges and line-of-sight rules are preserved.
- Crowd separation reuses flat buckets, removing per-cell allocation churn each frame. Tree sway and obstacle queries also include the spatial-index improvements made since v0.22.1.

## First-person view

- Rebuilt sword, shield, hands and forearms with camera-relative poses for movement, blocking and the attack chain.
- The shield counterbalances sword swings; rigid forearms connect cleanly to the hands.
- A camera-relative light keeps the weapon and gloves readable when facing the sun.
- The engaged enemy's health appears below the first-person reticle at close range, replacing an oversized world health bar.

## Verification tools

Startup also stops requesting two planned rival voice recordings that are not bundled yet; those reactions remain silent until recorded.

The performance harness can stage actual later nights and full skirmish armies, report frame-time percentiles after warmup, and track creature materials alongside mesh and entity counts. Reproducible CPU benchmarks and their limits are documented in [the performance notes](https://github.com/miskibin/warbell/blob/v0.23.0/docs/performance/v0.23.0.md).

The Windows installer is self-signed. Windows SmartScreen may show **Unknown Publisher**; choose **More info → Run anyway** to install.
